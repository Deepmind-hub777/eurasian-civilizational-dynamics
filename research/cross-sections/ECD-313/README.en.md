# ECD-313 | Eurasian Civilizational Cross-Section around 313 CE

**Status:** Research draft v0.1  
**Observation window:** c. 307–317 CE; 313 CE is the shared reference coordinate.

## Research question

How did political rupture and policy change in different Eurasian regions interact with the movement of people, goods, information, and religious institutions? Which networks persisted, shifted, or reorganized—and where does the surviving evidence remain insufficient?

This is a comparative time-slice, not a claim that four regions experienced one connected event in 313.

## Regional observations

### China
The fall of Luoyang in 311 and Chang'an in 316 ended the Western Jin regime. Sima Rui established the Eastern Jin political regime at Jiankang in 317 and formally took the throne in 318. Population movement southward mattered, but should not be described as the wholesale transfer of northern civilization into an otherwise empty south.

### Sogdiana and the Hexi Corridor
Sogdian Ancient Letter 2 (British Library Or. 8212/95), written by Nanai-vandak in northwestern China and addressed to partners in Samarkand, preserves news about upheaval in China, including the fall of Luoyang. Its date is variously presented as 312–313, approximately June/July 313, or 313–314 in a collection catalogue. This variation is retained rather than artificially resolved.

The letter is direct evidence for Sogdian commercial correspondence and the circulation of political information. The Sogdian term `xwn`, often translated as Huns/Xiongnu-related, does not by itself prove that the Xiongnu and European Huns were the same people or political entity. Nor does the letter establish a direct link between its author and Christian networks.

### Roman Empire
The policy associated with Constantine and Licinius in 313 granted religious toleration and provided for the restoration of Christian meeting places and property. Christianity had existed since the first century; 313 was not its beginning. The policy should not be conflated with the Edict of Thessalonica in 380, a major milestone in the imperial privileging of Nicene Christianity.

Archaeological interpretations of the Christian building at Dura-Europos have been reassessed in recent scholarship. The traditional “house church” model should therefore be cited with chronological and interpretive qualifications.

### Eurasian steppe
This section remains deliberately open. The Xiongnu, Huns, Chionites, Kidarites, and Hephthalites should not be treated as interchangeable labels. Linguistic resemblance, political continuity, population movement, and genetic ancestry are separate questions. Recent ancient-genome research adds evidence for trans-Eurasian connections but does not establish a simple, uninterrupted migration route.

## TCHS application

The case compares simultaneity, multiple dimensions, and multiple scales: individuals, households, cities, networks, and empires. Events outside 307–317 CE are explicitly marked as background or subsequent developments rather than silently folded into the year 313.

## DSON variables

The six variables are used as research questions, not scored conclusions:

- **N — Node Autonomy**
- **C — Connectivity and Redundancy**
- **T — Trust and Norms**
- **F — Flow of people, goods, capital, information, and knowledge**
- **R — Resilience**
- **A — Adaptation and Reorganization**

DSON remains a falsifiable analytical hypothesis, not an established law. Decentralization does not mean an absence of centrality; hubs may improve efficiency while also creating single points of failure.

## Preliminary synthesis

The strongest current finding is not that four civilizations were directly connected in 313. Rather, the evidence shows different forms of change within a shared observation window: political re-centering in China, long-distance commercial information exchange among Sogdians, a shift in Roman religious policy, and an incompletely documented steppe context.

The case supports further testing of how imperial order and transregional networks interact. It does not yet prove DSON as a general theory or establish direct links among Sogdian commercial, Christian, and European Hunnic networks.

## Evidence policy

Separate:
- **A:** primary evidence;
- **B:** scholarly interpretation;
- **C:** testable historical hypotheses;
- **D:** comparative synthesis and models.

Models in layer D must never overwrite primary evidence in layer A. Uncertainty, counter-evidence, and revisions must remain visible.
