# Evidence and Source Protocol

**ECD Research Standard v0.1**

## 1. Purpose

This protocol makes research claims traceable, reviewable, and revisable. It prevents primary materials, scholarly interpretations, hypotheses, and visual or analytical models from being conflated.

## 2. Four-Layer Evidence Architecture

### Layer A: Primary Evidence
Manuscripts, inscriptions, excavated documents, archaeological artifacts, contemporary records, and other identifiable primary materials. Preserve repository, shelfmark, edition, plate, and stable identifiers where available.

### Layer B: Scholarly Interpretation
Critical editions, translations, excavation reports, dating studies, linguistic analyses, and scholarly books or articles. Record authorship, publication details, and precise page or section references.

### Layer C: Historical Hypotheses
Each hypothesis should state:
- The claim and its scope;
- Supporting evidence and its layer;
- Counter-evidence and evidence gaps;
- Competing explanations;
- Confidence level and rationale;
- New evidence that could revise or falsify it.

### Layer D: Synthesis Models
Timelines, maps, network diagrams, statistical analyses, and civilizational-dynamics models. Every model must link back to Layer A, B, and C sources; it must never replace primary evidence.

## 3. Claim Labels

Use the following labels where helpful:
- **Direct evidence:** Explicitly recorded by a source or directly shown by material evidence.
- **Indirect evidence:** Supported through multiple materials or a chain of reasoning.
- **Scholarly inference:** An interpretation proposed by a researcher from the evidence.
- **Hypothesis under test:** Not yet sufficiently supported and requiring further examination.
- **Unknown / insufficient evidence:** The available material does not permit a determination.

## 4. Minimum Citation Requirements

Where possible, each citation should include:
- Author/editor, title, publisher or holding institution;
- Publication year, edition, and volume;
- Page, paragraph, line, inscription number, or archival identifier;
- Original language and translator;
- Stable URL, DOI, or collection identifier, if available;
- Access date for web resources that may change.

Do not describe a secondary account as direct examination of a primary source unless that examination actually took place.

## 5. Preservation of Text and Data

- Keep original text, transliteration, translation, and editorial restoration distinct.
- Mark lacunae, restorations, variant readings, and disputed dates.
- Never silently repair primary material for narrative convenience.
- Preserve prior versions or revision history when data changes.
- Do not copy entire books, articles, or databases into the repository without permission.

## 6. Inferential Guardrails and Common Errors

- Chronological sequence is not causation.
- Similar names do not establish ethnic identity.
- Contemporaneity does not establish direct contact.
- Cultural similarity does not necessarily indicate one-way transmission.
- Network persistence does not prove uninterrupted institutions.
- Lack of surviving sources does not prove non-existence.
- A connection drawn by a model is not a historically verified relationship.

## 7. Uncertainty and Disputes

For disputed dates, place names, ethnonyms, textual readings, or routes, present major scholarly positions and explain their evidence. Where a determination is not possible, preserve competing hypotheses; do not substitute a vote or consensus count for evidentiary assessment.

## 8. Data Corrections

Every correction should include:
1. The previous record and revised content;
2. The reason for revision;
3. New or corrected sources;
4. Effects on related hypotheses, charts, or models;
5. Contributor and date.

## 9. Copyright and Use

Citation, linking, and bibliographic description do not grant permission to redistribute the underlying work. Contributors must check the rights status of texts, images, maps, and datasets. When rights are unclear, prefer bibliographic details, brief excerpts within applicable limits, and links to the source.
